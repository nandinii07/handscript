Step one

Fill in the template

Three pages, 191 boxes. Write one character in each box, exactly as you normally would. This is the only part that takes any real time.

Download Template (PDF) I have filled it in

Printing

  • Print all three pages. Page 1 is letters and digits, page 2 is Greek and operators, page 3 is the rest.
  • Print at 100% scale — not "fit to page". Scaling shrinks the boxes and leaves you less room to write.
  • Plain white A4 or Letter, one page per sheet.

Writing

  • Use a dark pen with a reasonably thick stroke. Pencil and very light ink can be too faint to pick up.
  • Write inside each box, and try not to touch the printed border — ink crossing a border can confuse the box detection.
  • The small character printed under each box tells you what to write in it. Sit letters on the faint grey line.
  • Do not skip any box. A skipped box becomes a blank character in your font.
  • Leave the two crossed-out boxes on page 3 empty — but keep them in the picture when you scan. They are how we tell page 3 from page 1.
About the Greek capitals on page 3 Rows 2 to 4 of page 3 ask for Greek capitals, and fourteen of them look exactly like Latin letters — Α Β Ε Ζ Η Ι Κ Μ Ν Ο Ρ Τ Υ Χ. They are different characters even though they are the same shape. Write them just as you wrote the Latin ones; if you skip them, those characters will be blank in your font.

Scanning

  • A scanner at 300 DPI is best. A phone works if you use a scanning app such as Adobe Scan or the Notes app, which straighten the page for you.
  • Keep the page flat and the whole page in frame, including all four corners and the heading at the top.
  • Do not crop the edges or the heading — the heading is how we detect an upside-down page.
  • Even lighting, no shadow across the sheet, no glare.
  • Upload them in order: page 1, then 2, then 3. Pages 1 and 3 look alike, so getting them the wrong way round would put every character under the wrong letter. We check for this and will tell you.
